03 📐 EOQ — Economic Order Quantity

Core Formula

\[ Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}} \]

D = Annual demand (units/yr) · S = Ordering cost per order · H = Holding cost per unit per year

Key Derived Quantities

\[ N = \frac{D}{Q^*} \quad \text{(orders/year)} \] \[ \text{Avg Inventory} = \frac{Q^*}{2} \] \[ TC = \frac{DS}{Q} + \frac{QH}{2} \quad \text{(total annual cost)} \]
💡 Golden Rule At EOQ, Annual Ordering Cost = Annual Carrying Cost. They are always equal at Q*.

EOQ Graph Labels — MEMORISE (Q250)

Figure Labels A (Y-axis) = Cost · B (X-axis) = Quantity · C (U-curve top) = Total Cost
D (falling curve) = Order cost · E (rising line) = Carrying cost · F (X-axis min point) = EOQ (Q*)

✅ Answer: A=Cost, B=Qty, C=Total cost, D=Order cost, E=Carrying cost, F=EOQ(Q*)

EOQ Error Calculation (Q249 & Q163)

\[ \text{Error (\%)} = \frac{Q_{estimated} - Q_{actual}}{Q_{actual}} \times 100 \]

Worked example (same in both papers):

ParameterEstimateActual
Annual demand (D)1000 units2000 units
Holding cost (H)$10$20
Ordering cost (S)$50$25
\[ Q_{est} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1000 \times 50}{10}} = \sqrt{10000} = 100 \] \[ Q_{act} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 2000 \times 25}{20}} = \sqrt{5000} \approx 70.71 \] \[ \text{Error} = \frac{100 - 70.71}{70.71} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{41.42\%} \]
✅ Answer~41 (acceptable range: 40–42)

04 🏭 Slipline Co. — Full EOQ Problem (Q261–265)

Given: Demand = 1500 units/month → D = 18,000/yr · Unit cost = Rs.27 · S = Rs.150 · Carrying = 20% of unit cost → H = 0.20 × 27 = Rs.5.4/unit/yr

\[ Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 18000 \times 150}{5.4}} = \sqrt{1{,}000{,}000} = \mathbf{1000 \text{ units}} \] \[ N = \frac{18000}{1000} = \mathbf{18 \text{ orders/yr}} \] \[ \text{Annual Ordering Cost} = 18 \times 150 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 2700} \] \[ \text{Avg Inventory} = \frac{1000}{2} = \mathbf{500 \text{ units}} \] \[ \text{Annual Carrying Cost} = 500 \times 5.4 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 2700} \]

05 🎯 Newsvendor Model (Critical Ratio)

Used for single-period, uncertain demand (perishable goods, seasonal items). Order only once before demand is known.

Formula

\[ CR = \frac{C_u}{C_u + C_o} \] \[ Q^* = a + CR \times (b - a) \quad \text{for Uniform}[a, b] \text{ demand} \]

C_u = understock cost = p − c (lost profit) · C_o = overstock cost = c − s (loss on unsold unit)
p = selling price · c = unit cost · s = salvage value

Left / Right Rule

ConditionCRQ* location
C_o < C_u (overstock cheaper)> 0.5RIGHT of mean ✅
C_o > C_u (understock cheaper)< 0.5LEFT of mean
C_o = C_u= 0.5AT the mean

Ramu's Diya Problem (Q258–260)

Cost = Rs.1 · Selling price = Rs.2 · Salvage = Rs.0.50 · Demand ~ Uniform[800, 1000]

\[ C_u = 2 - 1 = 1, \quad C_o = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 \] \[ CR = \frac{1}{1 + 0.5} = \frac{1}{1.5} \approx \mathbf{0.67} \] \[ Q^* = 800 + 0.67 \times 200 \approx \mathbf{933 \text{ units}} \]
✅ AnswersCR ≈ 0.67 · Q* ≈ 933 · C_o < C_u so Q* is to the RIGHT of mean (900)

06 ✈️ Revenue Management — Booking Limit & Protection Level

Used in airlines/hotels to decide how many seats to protect for high-fare passengers.

TermDefinition
Protection level (y*)Seats reserved for high-fare (business) passengers
Booking limit (b*)Max seats sold to low-fare (economy) = Total − y*

Formula

\[ CR = 1 - \frac{r_L}{r_H} \] \[ y^* = a + CR \times (b - a) \quad \text{(protection level, high-fare demand} \sim U[a,b]) \] \[ \text{Booking limit} = \text{Total seats} - y^* \]

r_L = low-fare revenue · r_H = high-fare revenue

🔗 Same Formula — Two Faces The revenue management CR is the same newsvendor formula with C_u = r_H − r_L and C_o = r_L.
Substituting: C_u/(C_u+C_o) = (r_H−r_L)/r_H = 1 − r_L/r_H ✅

Flight Problem (Q164–165)

100 total seats · Economy = Rs.2500 · Business = Rs.7500 · Business demand ~ Uniform[10, 20]

\[ CR = 1 - \frac{2500}{7500} = 1 - 0.333 = 0.667 \] \[ y^* = 10 + 0.667 \times 10 \approx \mathbf{17 \text{ seats protected}} \] \[ b^* = 100 - 17 = \mathbf{83 \text{ (booking limit)}} \]

"Increased tourist footfall towards end of year" → Seasonal (NOT cyclic)

Two Primary Tasks of Time Series Forecasting (Q256)

  1. ✅ Identifying patterns in the data
  2. ✅ Discovering relationships amongst variables of interest

❌ "Identify variables of interest" alone is NOT a primary task of time series forecasting.

Exponential Smoothing (Q254)

\[ F_{t+1} = \alpha D_t + (1 - \alpha) F_t \]

F_t = Forecast period t · D_t = Actual demand period t · α = smoothing constant (0<α<1)
Higher α → more responsive to recent data · Lower α → smoother forecast

The other formula in exam — F_t = μ − θ·ε_{t-1} — is an ARMA/Moving Average model, NOT exponential smoothing.

10 📋 Order-Up-To Level (Q172)

Also called Base-stock policy. Used in periodic review inventory systems.

\[ \text{Order-Up-To Level} = \text{Demand forecast during } (L + T) + \text{Safety stock} \] \[ \text{Order qty} = \text{OUL} - \text{On-hand inventory} - \text{On-order inventory} \]

L = Lead time · T = Review period

✅ All 4 inputs required (Q172 — select ALL) Sales forecast · Lead time · On-hand inventory · Safety stock

11 🏷️ Sunk Cost — Irrelevant to Inventory Decisions (Q171)

A sunk cost is a cost already incurred that cannot be recovered. It must be ignored in future decisions.

✅ Irrelevant cost (correct answer Q171) "A company bought a machine that broke and could not be returned" = Sunk cost = IRRELEVANT to inventory management.

The following ARE relevant inventory costs and should NOT be ignored:

  • Cost of processing an order from supplier (ordering cost)
  • Transportation cost from supplier to manufacturing site (ordering cost)
  • Carrying cost for holding items (holding cost)

13 🤖 K-Means Clustering (Q161)

Algorithm Steps

  1. Choose k initial centroids (randomly or by heuristic)
  2. Assign each data point to nearest centroid (by Euclidean distance)
  3. Recompute each centroid as mean of all assigned points
  4. Repeat steps 2–3 until centroids stop moving (convergence)

Properties — True / False (Q161)

StatementExam Answer
Requires initial centroids to be chosen randomly✅ TRUE
May not find the globally optimal solution❌ FALSE (per exam key)
Complexity is polynomial w.r.t. number of data points & clusters✅ TRUE
Can be used for higher-dimensional spaces✅ TRUE
✅ Correct TRUE answers (Q161) Random initial centroids · Polynomial complexity · Works in higher dimensions

Centroid Calculation Example

Points: (1,1), (2,3), (3,2) and (8,8), (7,6), (9,7) with k=2:

Cluster 1 centroid = ((1+2+3)/3, (1+3+2)/3) = (2, 2)
Cluster 2 centroid = ((8+7+9)/3, (8+6+7)/3) = (8, 7)
Iterate until centroids don't change.

K-Means Result from Exam (paper before Q161)

Correct answer: 2 clusters, centroids (1.5, 2.5) and (7, 7)
The number of iterations = 2
